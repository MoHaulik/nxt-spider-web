## About the project (under 500 words)
```
## Inspiration
Everyone has stopped to watch a spider come down on a single thread. We wanted that moment in mixed reality, treated like cinema rather than a toy. No words, menus or score in the world. Your room dims, something glints on your real ceiling, and a small spider lowers itself until it hangs at arm's length in front of you. Then it is yours.

## What it does
Point, and the spider swings there on its dragline. Swing it toward a real wall, the floor or your sofa and it casts a line that zips out and ties there: the scaffold, tied to your room, so every room gives a different web. Tap your fingers to start a thread, tap again to tie it off. Threads across the middle become radii through a hub, and the spider lays the sticky spiral where you sweep it, as orb-weavers really build.

You don't chase flies; the web catches them, and your real hands help. A fly veers from your hand, a sweep shoos it into the silk, and if you hold a finger still a fly may land on it: flick, and it flies into the web. Pinch one from the air and feed your spider by hand. With every meal it grows.

Then the story turns. Rain falls around you; drops swell on your ceiling and let go, so you dodge. When the sun returns, every thread is beaded with dew, each drop a tiny lens. A bird calls; hold still and it passes. After the right meal, a place at the top of your web lights up: swing the spider there and it lays its eggs, wrapping the sac in silk. After rain, in the sun, the sac opens and spiderlings balloon out to real surfaces in your room and build their own small webs.

## A reason to come back
The web stays anchored where you left it. While you're away the spider wraps the flies it catches and saves them for you, and its families grow. Real time of day sets the light: dewy gold mornings, amber evenings, moonlit nights. The landing page says one line, your chapter: Build a home. Maintain a web. Grow a family.

## How we built it
An NXT WebXR original: it runs from a link in the Quest browser, with no install. Built on Meta's Immersive Web SDK with three.js; everything procedural. The web is a position-based (Verlet) simulation of thousands of silk particles that sag, snap and are repaired. Plane detection ties the web and the colonies to your real room, persistent anchors keep it there, and hand occluders let your real hands pass in front of the silk. All sound is synthesized; plucking the web plays it. Hands-first and seated: everything is reachable within two feet, and the first payoff, a catch, comes inside a minute.

## What's next
More species and web architectures, seasons, and shared rooms where two people's spiders build together, on the Meta Horizon Store.
```

## Hand interactions (if the form asks separately)
```
Hands only, seated, no controller and no menus.
- Point to steer: raise your hand and point; the spider goes where you point on the web.
- Swing to tie: at the start, swing the spider toward a wall, the floor or furniture and it casts a line that zips out and ties there; you never lose control.
- Tap your fingers: a thread begins at the spider. Swing, then tap again to tie it off (holding the pinch also works). Either hand can tap while the other steers.
- Sweep to lay the spiral: the sticky spiral is laid where the spider is.
- Touch a caught fly with the spider: it is taken and eaten.
- Shoo flies: a fly veers away from your hand; sweep your hand to drive it into the web.
- Hold a finger still: a fly may land on it. Flick it into the web.
- Pinch a fly out of the air and hold it, buzzing; let go at the web to feed your spider by hand.
- Poke a fly caught in the web: it struggles; poke it three times and it tears free. You can poke the web from your seat: jab at what your fingertip covers.
- Touch the spider: it flinches, then lifts a leg to you.
- Dodge and freeze: move out from under a falling drop; hold still when the bird calls.
- Lower your hand: the spider goes about its own business (fetches prey, goes to its nest).
- Pluck a thread with your fingertip: it rings and sways.
The first time it is needed, a soft cue at your fingertips shows the pinch.
```
